A week of dinners from one veg box

adminPublished October 12, 20262 min read3 views

A veg box is a lovely idea until Thursday, when half of it is still in the crisper drawer. This is the plan we use to get through a whole box before the next one arrives, with the most delicate things first.

Contents
  1. Sort it the day it arrives
  2. The week, roughly
  3. The vegetable nobody asked for
  4. Keep a list on the fridge

Sort it the day it arrives

Unpack everything onto the counter and make three piles: what wilts in two days, what keeps for a week, and what keeps for a month. Herbs and salad leaves go first; roots and squash wait their turn.

The week, roughly

  1. Monday: a big salad with the most fragile leaves and herbs.
  2. Tuesday: the tomatoes go into a quick sauce, before they soften.
  3. Wednesday: greens wilted into pasta or eggs.
  4. Thursday: a tray of roasted roots, half of it saved for Friday.
  5. Friday: the leftover roots in a soup with a tin of beans.
  6. Saturday: something slow with the squash.
  7. Sunday: a fridge-clearing frittata.

The vegetable nobody asked for

Every box has one: the kohlrabi, the celeriac, the third cabbage in a row. Roast it, grate it into a slaw, or turn it into soup. If all else fails, it goes into Friday's soup and nobody notices.

Keep a list on the fridge

Write what is left on a sticky note on the fridge door and cross things off as you go. It takes ten seconds a day, and it is the single thing that stopped us throwing vegetables away.
